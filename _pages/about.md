I teach machines to see things people cannot — methane escaping a pipe in infrared, a hairline crack in railway steel at 30 km/h. That work has taken me from Yangon to Bangkok, and this September it takes me to Chiayi, Taiwan, for a fully funded master's at National Chung Cheng University.

Before that I spent a year at KMUTT's Maintenance Technology Center building the data pipelines and anomaly-detection models behind industrial inspection research. I care most about the unglamorous part: making perception systems reliable enough to trust on real hardware, outside the lab.

Outside of research I mentor robotics teams, cook a lot, and am still working out how to explain any of this at a dinner party. More on [that side of things](/beyond/), or see my [CV](/cv/) for the full record.
