I teach machines to see things people cannot: methane escaping a pipe in infrared, a hairline crack in railway steel at 30 km/h. In September 2026 I start a fully funded Master's in Mechanical Engineering at [National Chung Cheng University](https://deptime.ccu.edu.tw/?Lang=en) in Taiwan, working on perception and robotic autonomy.

Before that I spent a year as a machine learning researcher at KMUTT's Maintenance Technology Center, building data pipelines and anomaly detection for industrial inspection. I care most about the unglamorous part, making perception systems reliable enough to trust on real hardware, outside the lab.

---

## How I got here

I started at Mandalay Technological University in 2014 and moved to Yangon Technological University the year after. Coursework was one part of it, but robotics was where I actually spent my time. In 2016 a few of us co-founded STA, the first student science and technology group at YTU, running tech talks and teaching Arduino workshops to younger students.

From there it turned into competitions. In 2017 we organized the first LEGO SUMO robotics contest at YTU. The next year it grew into the YTU Mini-Rolympics, and by 2019 it was a real tournament: fifteen teams, company sponsors, and coverage on local media.

2019 was the big year. I mentored Team Yangon at the Myanmar RoboLeague and we won the championship, which took us to the FIRST Global Challenge in Dubai. The team placed 82nd out of 189 countries and came home with the Judges Award. Meeting young engineers from all over the world there changed how I thought about what was possible back home.

That trip led to **L.O.R.A**, the Laboratory of Robotics and AI. I founded it in 2019 and it grew to about forty-four undergraduates, one of the first youth-run robotics research teams in Myanmar. When COVID hit we built disinfection robots and donated them to Wai Bar Gi Hospital in Yangon. It is still the work I am proudest of. Around the same time I taught computer science and robotics at ConceptX International School, and became Head of Department before I left.

Then things fell apart. The political situation made it impossible to continue, and I left my degree at YTU unfinished. For a stretch, the plan I had been building for years simply stopped.

So I started over. In 2023 I moved to Thailand and began an engineering degree again from the ground up at KMUTT, finishing in Automation Engineering in 2025. This time the research came together: my thesis on detecting methane leaks with an infrared camera grew into a paper that won Best Paper at IEEE ICoICT 2025, and I co-authored a journal paper on eddy current testing for railway inspection. Along the way I wrote SLAM code in C with CODIA Lab and Panasonic Singapore, worked on vehicle control with micro-ROS for a Formula Student project, built agentic LLM workflows at HoneyHunt.AI, and spent a year as a machine learning researcher at KMUTT's Maintenance Technology Center.

In September 2026 I start at National Chung Cheng University in Taiwan, focusing on perception and robotic autonomy. If any of this connects to what you do, I would be glad to hear from you.
